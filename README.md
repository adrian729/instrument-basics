# Instrument Basics

Fingering charts for woodwinds and brass: every note of an instrument's usual range, from the lowest up, at written pitch, each with the standard fingering first and a choice of alternatives under it (for tuning, soft or loud playing, response, or an easier passage). Each note is shown on the staff, with a diagram of the keys or valves and the same fingering in words, and what it sounds on a transposing instrument.

Live at https://adrian729.github.io/instrument-basics/.

The instruments, in the picker's sections and groups:

- Woodwinds
  - **Flutes**: the concert flute (B3, with a B foot, to F7) and the piccolo (D4 to D7, sounding an octave higher). Boehm-system, fingered alike for two octaves; the flute's foot joint keys shown in its diagrams.
  - **Clarinets**: the E♭ (written E3 to A6), B♭ and A (E3 to C7) and bass clarinets (C3 to D♯7), Boehm-system, fingered alike to clarion C but for the bass clarinet's lowest keys; its altissimo its own, with a half-hole. And the E♭, B♭ and A in the German (Oehler) system, as played in Germany and Austria, with their own keys, fingerings and drawing.
  - **Saxophones**: soprano, alto, tenor and baritone (written B♭3 to F♯6, and the baritone's low A), fingered alike. The altissimo is left out.
- Brass (every one in treble clef, and every three-valve one fingered like the trumpet, from the same chart)
  - **Trumpets**: the B♭ trumpet and the C trumpet (written F♯3 to C6), and the four-valve piccolo trumpet in B♭ (written C♯3 to G5, its 4th valve shown).
  - **Cornet** and **Flugelhorn**, each in B♭ (written F♯3 to C6): fingered like the trumpet at its pitch, but instruments of their own (conical, the flugelhorn a bugle's wide bore), so not among the trumpets.
  - **Mellophone** in F and **Tenor horn** in E♭ (the alto horn, in the US), each written F♯3 to C6.
  - **Baritone horn** (three valves, written F♯3 to C6) and **Euphonium** (four, compensating, its 4th on the side for the left hand, written C♯3 to G6), in B♭ treble clef, as brass bands read them.

A group is one instrument in its sizes or keys (the flutes, the clarinets, the saxophones, the trumpets), or an instrument alone. The picker, at the top of the page, is the group, from a list in sections that can grow long, then its kind among the group's, if it has more than one (each group comes back to the kind last chosen in it), and its key system where there are two (the clarinets' Boehm and German). The instrument is remembered, and in the address for a link to one (`?instrument=alto-sax`). Notes can be named with letters (with octave numbers) or in solfège, and the alternatives folded or shown. The compact view shows each note as its name and its standard fingering only, the diagram stood up and small, for an overview: about an octave on a screen.

## Run locally

It's built with Vite, TypeScript and Svelte (Node 24, pnpm):

```sh
pnpm install
pnpm dev          # http://localhost:5174, reloading as files change
pnpm build        # the site, in dist/
pnpm preview      # dist/, at http://localhost:8899
pnpm test         # the types (tsc, svelte-check), then the Playwright tests
```

The tests build the site and serve it on port 8899, then check its data, its behaviour and its looks (screenshots in `tests/screenshots/`, taken with Chrome on macOS; `pnpm test:update` retakes them after a change meant to change them). Pushing to `main` publishes the site to GitHub Pages (`.github/workflows/pages.yml`; the repo's Pages source must be "GitHub Actions").

## Code

- `src/notations/`: a notation per kind of instrument: how its fingerings are written in the data, drawn and said. `flute/` (the Boehm flute's keys: `"B 123 G# | --- Eb"`), `clarinet/` (the Boehm clarinet's: `"T R 123 E | 123"`), `oehler/` (the German-system clarinet's: `"T R 123 E | 123 F"`), `sax/` (`"T 12- | sBb ---"`), `valves/` (valved brass: `"13"`); `art.ts` and `art/` the instruments drawn for the key above each chart, each a generated file traced from a photo, with its keys labelled; each explained at the top of its `notation.ts`, and each diagram following one convention (`notations/types.ts`).
- `src/data/`: the instruments, by section. `types.ts` has what every notation's data shares, `instrument.ts` what an instrument and a group are, `instruments.ts` lists the groups in their sections. `woodwinds/` and `brass/` have a file per group (`flutes.ts`, `clarinets.ts`, `saxophones.ts`, `trumpets.ts`, `cornet.ts`, `flugelhorn.ts`, `mellophone.ts`, `tenor-horn.ts`, `baritone-horn.ts`, `euphonium.ts`: each instrument's transposition, what to know, the footer's tip and credits) and the charts they read: every note and its fingerings, an instrument's own or shared (`flute.ts`, `piccolo.ts`, `clarinet.ts` for every Boehm clarinet, `oehler.ts` for every German one, `sax.ts` for every saxophone; `three-valves.ts` for every three-valve brass instrument, `piccolo-trumpet.ts`, `euphonium-chart.ts`); `brass/brass.ts` has what the brass groups share. Checked when the tests run, so a typo fails them.
- `src/lib/notes.ts`: notes' names (letters or solfège), what's heard of a written note (from the transposition, in letters and semitones), and where a note sits on the staff.
- `src/lib/store.svelte.ts`: the switches' choices (instrument, names, view, alternatives), saved in the browser. `src/ui/SwitchBar.svelte` shows them; `src/views/controller.svelte.ts` makes the chart follow them.
- `src/views/`: the chart (`chart.ts`, made again when the instrument changes, its text too), the staff (`staff.ts`), drawn as SVG, and the compact view's diagrams (`vertical.ts`: any notation's diagram stood up).
- `src/views/glyphs.ts`: the clef, sharp, flat and whole note, as paths from Bravura (the SMuFL reference font), with its engraving defaults (the lines' thickness, the ledger lines' reach), made by `scripts/glyphs.py` (its docstring says how to run it).
- `src/styles.css`: the styles: ducktape-colors' colours and DM Mono, with a print layout.

### Adding an instrument

1. Its notation: one of `src/notations/`, or a new one implementing `Notation` (a horn's thumb valve would be a token added to `valves/`).
2. Its data: a file of registers in that notation (or the one it shares), from a source to credit.
3. Its definition, in its group's file (or a new group, in its section's folder, added to `instruments.ts`): id (a permanent URL), name, kind, transposition, clef (only treble is drawn yet; a bass-clef instrument needs `staff.ts` to learn it), options for its diagram, what to know, tip.
4. Its id in `InstrumentId` (`instrument.ts`), its range in the data test, and a screenshot.

## Sources

- **Flutes**: [The Woodwind Fingering Guide](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/flute/), the comments adapted: its flute pages ([first octave](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/flute/fl_alt_1.html), [second](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/flute/fl_alt_2.html), [third](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/flute/fl_alt_3.html), [fourth](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/flute/fl_alt_4.html)) and piccolo pages ([first octave](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/flute/picc_alt_1.html), [second](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/flute/picc_alt_2.html), [third](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/flute/picc_alt_3.html)). A choice of the guide's alternatives, one or a few for each purpose, not all of them; never those that need a half-covered hole or the C♯ trill key some flutes have, those meant for the bass or alto flute, or the piccolo's that close its end with the right little finger. Where the keys sit in the diagrams follows the guide's labelled photos ([flute](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/flute/fl_fing.html)).
- **Clarinets**: [The Woodwind Fingering Guide](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/clarinet/)'s Boehm clarinet pages, the comments adapted: basic ([chalumeau](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/clarinet/cl_bas_1.html), [clarion](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/clarinet/cl_bas_2.html), [lower altissimo](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/clarinet/cl_bas_3.html)), its fingerings and alternatives; a few from the alternate chart for the throat notes' tone ([chalumeau and throat](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/clarinet/cl_alt_1.html)); the bass clarinet's lowest notes and altissimo from its own pages ([chalumeau](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/clarinet/basscl_alt_1.html), [altissimo](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/clarinet/basscl_alt_2.html)), the first listed. Where the keys sit in the diagrams follows the guide's labelled photo ([clarinet](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/clarinet/fing_clsop.html)); the ranges and transpositions from Wikipedia's [Clarinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarinet) and [Bass clarinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bass_clarinet). The German (Oehler) system's fingerings are from the guide's Oehler/Albert pages (basic: [chalumeau](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/clarinet/ocl_bas_1.html), [clarion](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/clarinet/ocl_bas_2.html), [altissimo](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/clarinet/ocl_bas_3.html); alternate: [upper altissimo](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/clarinet/ocl_alt_4.html)), checked against [Yamaha's German-system clarinet owner's manual](https://data.yamaha.com/files/download/other_assets/5/331715/G_Clarinet_2013_web.pdf) and [A-Klarinette's fingering chart](https://a-klarinette.de/files/A-KLARINETTE/content/dokumente/Klarinette_Grifftabelle.pdf), which agree with it to F6 (at G6 they put left 1 with the right hand first, as here); its history and keys from Wikipedia's [Oehler system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oehler_system) and [Oehler-System](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oehler-System) (German).
- **Trumpets and the other three-valve brass**: [Yamaha's trumpet fingering chart](https://www.yamaha.com/en/musical_instrument_guide/common/images/trumpet/fingering.pdf), standard and alternatives; the tuning advice, the standard range and the 4th valve from Wikipedia's [Trumpet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trumpet), the piccolo trumpet's range from its [Piccolo trumpet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piccolo_trumpet) (Herbert, Myers and Wallace). The piccolo trumpet's low 4th-valve fingerings are the valves' arithmetic, checked by a test (every fingering a harmonic of the open horn). That the cornet, flugelhorn, mellophone, tenor horn and baritone horn are fingered like the trumpet, and their ranges, from Wikipedia's [Cornet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornet), [Flugelhorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flugelhorn), [Mellophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellophone), [Tenor horn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenor_horn) and [Baritone horn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baritone_horn).
- **Euphonium**: Micah Everett's [chromatic fingering chart for euphonium (treble clef)](https://olemiss.edu/lowbrass/lowbrassmethods/charts/euphoniumtcfingering.pdf) (University of Mississippi), its fingerings in its order of preference, and its non-compensating ones in the facts; the range and the compensating 4th valve from Wikipedia's [Euphonium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphonium) (Herbert, Myers and Wallace); that it's on the side, under the left index finger, from it and [Brass Techniques and Pedagogy](https://pressbooks.palni.org/brasstechniquesandpedagogy/chapter/the-euphonium/).
- **Drawings**: every instrument's, in the key above its chart, is traced by hand from a maker's photo on Wikimedia Commons: Yamaha Corporation's ([YTR-8335LA](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yamaha_Trumpet_YTR-8335LA.tif), [YTR-9835](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yamaha_Piccolo_trumpet_YTR-9835.jpg), [YCR-6330II](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yamaha_Cornet_YCR-6330II.tif), [YFH-8310Z](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yamaha_Flugelhorn_YFH-8310Z.tif), [YMP-204MS](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yamaha_Mellophone_YMP-204MS.tiff), [YAH-203](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yamaha_Alto_horn_YAH-203.tif), [YBH-301](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yamaha_Baritone_horn_YBH-301.tif), [YEP-621](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yamaha_Euphonium_YEP-621.tif), [YFL-482H](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yamaha_Flute_YFL-482H.tif), [YPC-82](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yamaha_Piccolo_YPC-82.png), [YCL-CSGIII](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yamaha_Clarinet_YCL-CSGIII.tif), [YCL-622II](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yamaha_Bass_Clarinet_YCL-622II.jpg), [YCL-457II-22](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yamaha_Clarinet_YCL-457II-22_%288K%29.jpg), [YSS-875EX](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yamaha_Saxophone_YSS-875_EX.jpg), [YAS-62](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yamaha_Saxophone_YAS-62.png), [YTS-62](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yamaha_Saxophone_YTS-62_%28recrop%29.png)) and Amati Kraslice's ([baritone saxophone](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Baritone_Saxophone_Amati.png)), [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/), and is shared under the same licence (`src/notations/art/`). Every key or valve the diagrams use is labelled where it really is; a long instrument has a second, upright layout for phones. Valves are numbered as the casings are engraved. - **Saxophones**: [The Woodwind Fingering Guide](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/sax/)'s saxophone pages, the comments adapted: basic ([first octave](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/sax/sax_bas_1.html), [second](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/sax/sax_bas_2.html)) and alternate ([first octave](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/sax/sax_alt_1.html), [second](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/sax/sax_alt_2.html), [lower altissimo](https://www.wfg.woodwind.org/sax/sax_alt_4.html)).
