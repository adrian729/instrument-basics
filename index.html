Every fingering for the piccolo

Every note from the lowest D to the top, the standard fingering first and the alternatives under it, for tuning, soft or loud playing, and response. For the Boehm piccolo; the first two octaves are fingered as on the flute, the third differs on a few notes.

  • There's no foot joint. No piccolo has one: its lowest note is D, all six fingers down, and the flute's low C♯, C and B keys aren't needed for anything.
  • It sounds an octave higher. Piccolo music is written an octave below what's heard. The chart names written notes, and each card says what you'll hear.
  • The right pinky stays down on the E♭ key for almost every note. The diagrams show the few where it's lifted, as for low D and middle D.

Reading the diagrams

  • The piccolo as you see it playing: the mouthpiece on the left, the left hand nearer it, the right hand further out.
  • Dark is pressed, white is up. The numbers are your fingers: 1 the index, 2 the middle, 3 the ring finger.
  • The thumb has two keys, under the piccolo. Use B; a few fingerings ask for the B♭ lever.
  • The pinkies' keys: G♯ for the left, E♭ for the right.
  • The dashed D and D♯ are the trill keys, between your right-hand fingers, pressed with whichever finger is free. The side B♭ lever, beside right 1, is pressed with the side of that finger. Only a few notes use these.
  • Under each diagram the same fingering in words. Open-hole and closed-hole piccolos are fingered alike.

The piccolo tends to be sharp low down and flat at the very top: most alternatives are for that, or for soft and loud playing, or to make a note speak. Try them with a tuner, and keep what works on yours.
